If you picked up a discounted bottle of Regaine this week, the honest answer is that nothing will visibly change for several months, and for some men it may not change much at all. That is not pessimism, it is what the clinical patient information says plainly. This week's Black Friday prices have put minoxidil in a lot of bathroom cabinets that did not have it last week. Before you build your expectations around what you have read in reviews, it is worth knowing what the evidence actually says happens next.

## Why is everyone buying minoxidil this week?

Because it is cheap right now. According to [LADbible's roundup of this week's Black Friday deals](https://www.ladbible.com/lifestyle/men-hair-growth-treatment-black-friday-deals-867393-20261121), Regaine is one treatment many swear by, and several of its products are currently discounted on Amazon for Black Friday, with price cuts of up to 44 percent on the solution and foam ranges.

Reviews quoted alongside those deals are glowing. One shopper is quoted as saying that after two months of use, "I can see that the hair is starting to grow back." Another reportedly described new hairline growth appearing "after a few days." These are real experiences for the people who wrote them, but they are also exactly the kind of anecdote that sets unrealistic expectations for everyone else, because results vary enormously and nobody posts a review on month three when nothing has happened yet.

The product itself is not mysterious. The same article explains that minoxidil is the active ingredient in Regaine and is used to help treat hereditary pattern hair loss, and that applied directly to the scalp, it is thought to increase blood flow to hair follicles, helping to stimulate hair growth and keep follicles in their growing phase for longer. What matters is not what it is, but how long it takes and what it can realistically do, and that is where the clinical information is far more useful than a star rating.

## How long does minoxidil actually take to work?

At least 6 months. That is not our figure, it is the figure given in the [British Association of Dermatologists' patient leaflet on male pattern hair loss](https://cdn.bad.org.uk/uploads/2021/11/29200304/Male-pattern-hair-loss-Update-March-2016-lay-reviewed-March-20166.pdf), which states that minoxidil needs to be used for at least 6 months before any benefit may be noted.

Six months of twice daily application, through Christmas, into spring, before you can fairly judge whether it is doing anything. That is a long window to hold onto hope with no feedback, particularly if the reason you bought it was a stressful moment looking in the mirror rather than a calm, planned decision.

The same leaflet is specific about what minoxidil does when it does work. It explains that applying 5% minoxidil liquid or foam to the scalp may help to slow down the progression of hair loss and partially restore hair. Slow down and partially restore. Not reverse, not cure, not guarantee. That gap between "partially restore" and the dramatic before and after photos used in marketing is where most of the disappointment happens later.

If you want the fuller picture on the research behind minoxidil, including what the clinical trials actually measured, we have gone through that in detail in [our evidence based look at whether minoxidil regrows hair](/blog/does-minoxidil-regrow-hair-evidence).

## Why does some hair fall out before it gets better?

This is the part almost nobody warns you about when they hand you a discounted bottle. In the early weeks, things can look worse before they look better.

The [BAD leaflet](https://cdn.bad.org.uk/uploads/2021/11/29200304/Male-pattern-hair-loss-Update-March-2016-lay-reviewed-March-20166.pdf) is direct about this: minoxidil solution may cause an initial hair fall in the first 2-8 weeks of treatment, and this usually subsides when the new hairs start to grow. If you did not know to expect this, it can feel like proof the product is making things worse, and it is a common reason people stop in week three, right before anything useful would have happened anyway.

There are also simple practical warnings worth repeating. The same leaflet notes that minoxidil can cause skin reactions such as dryness, redness, scaling and/or itchiness at the site of application and should not be applied if there are cuts or open wounds. The [LADbible coverage of this week's deals](https://www.ladbible.com/lifestyle/men-hair-growth-treatment-black-friday-deals-867393-20261121) adds a basic safety note too, confirming that the solution is also flammable, so keep it away from naked flames and heat sources. None of that is scaremongering, it is the sort of detail that gets lost when a product is being sold on a 41 percent off banner rather than a patient information leaflet.

## What happens if it works, and then you stop?

This is the detail that changes the whole calculation for a lot of men, and it rarely makes it into the marketing. The [BAD leaflet](https://cdn.bad.org.uk/uploads/2021/11/29200304/Male-pattern-hair-loss-Update-March-2016-lay-reviewed-March-20166.pdf) states plainly that any benefit is only maintained for as long as the treatment is used, and later repeats that it is important to note that all of the topical and oral treatments only work for as long as the treatment is continued.

In practice, that means minoxidil is not something you take for six months and then stop once you are happy. It is a maintenance commitment, potentially for years, and the underlying hair loss is still there the entire time, simply being held back rather than resolved. The leaflet is equally clear that male pattern hair loss itself tends to progress very slowly, from several years to decades, which is some reassurance about pace, but also confirms there is no finish line where the treatment can be safely stopped.

That is a very different proposition from what a half price bottle implies. It is not a one off fix you buy in a sale. It is the start of an ongoing routine, and whether that routine suits your life is worth thinking through properly before you are six months in.

## What can minoxidil not do, and who is it realistically for?

The leaflet does not describe minoxidil as a cure, because it is not one. Asked directly, can male pattern hair loss be cured, the same source answers: no, there is no cure. Minoxidil sits alongside finasteride as one of the two licensed treatments that may help, and the leaflet notes that finasteride tablets reduce levels of dihydrotestosterone, which may slow hair loss and possibly help regrowth of hair, continuous use for 3 to 6 months being needed before benefit is usually seen. Finasteride carries its own, separate safety considerations that have had recent regulatory attention, which we cover in [our piece on the finasteride MHRA safety warning](/blog/finasteride-mhra-safety-warning-2026) if that is a route you are weighing up alongside minoxidil.

For some men minoxidil genuinely helps and is worth the ongoing routine. For others, it does little, the skin reaction makes daily use unworkable, or six months is simply too long to wait with an important event, a new job, or a wedding on the calendar. None of that is a failure on your part. It is just a mismatch between what the product offers and what you actually need right now, which is coverage you can see today rather than a possible partial improvement by next summer.

## What is the honest alternative if minoxidil is not for you?

If you want visible density now, rather than a maybe in six months, a hair system does a different job entirely. It is not a medical treatment and it will not change what is happening underneath your scalp, but it gives you a full head of hair the same day you are fitted, which the [BAD leaflet](https://cdn.bad.org.uk/uploads/2021/11/29200304/Male-pattern-hair-loss-Update-March-2016-lay-reviewed-March-20166.pdf) itself acknowledges is the long standing appeal of hairpieces, noting that some affected individuals find wigs, toupees and even hair extensions very helpful in disguising hair loss.

Modern hair systems used today bear little resemblance to the synthetic toupees that leaflet is describing. They are built and colour matched individually, and they need [regular maintenance every few weeks](/hair-system-maintenance) rather than being something you forget about once fitted, in much the same way minoxidil needs daily discipline rather than being a one off purchase. The difference is the result is visible immediately, not conditional on six months of consistent use and an uncertain outcome.

This matters most for two groups. Men who have already given minoxidil an honest six month trial and seen little change, and men who simply do not want to spend a winter waiting to find out. ## How do you know when it is time to stop waiting and come in?

There is no clinical reason to stop minoxidil to have a hair system fitted, and no reason you cannot run both tracks at once if minoxidil is still within its six month trial window and you want cover in the meantime. The two are not competing treatments, they solve different problems on different timescales.

The practical markers worth watching for are these:

- You are past six months of consistent, correct use with no change you can see in photographs, not memory
- The skin reaction has made daily application impractical
- You have an event, job change or simply a point in life where waiting is not realistic
- You would rather spend the money and effort on something with an immediate, visible result
